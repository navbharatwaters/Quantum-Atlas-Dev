# Contract Intelligence for Legal Teams: A Practical Implementation Guide

## Abstract (for the download page)

Contract review is slow because the information is buried in long documents with inconsistent wording. This guide explains how contract intelligence systems work: how documents are read, how clauses and obligations are extracted, how a contract collection can be searched with natural-language questions, and how every output stays traceable to a page of the source. It is aimed at in-house legal teams, law firm technology leads and compliance officers. It is explicit about the limits. These systems prepare and organise information for lawyers; they do not give legal advice, and review by a qualified person remains part of the process.

Length target: 12 to 16 pages. Format: PDF, with a one-page evaluation worksheet.

## Outline

1. **Scope and caution** (half page)
   - What the technology does and does not do. Not legal advice.

2. **Where the time goes today** (1 page)
   - Review, due diligence, NDA triage, renewals and obligation tracking. Describe tasks, not statistics.

3. **How a contract is read by software** (2 pages)
   - Scans and native files, text recognition, layout, page and section structure.
   - Splitting bundles (a contract with annexes and amendments).
   - Why traceability to the page matters.

4. **Clause extraction** (2 pages)
   - Identifying clause types; extracting parties, dates, amounts, governing law, term and termination.
   - Handling amendments and conflicting versions.
   - Blank over wrong: leaving a field empty when unsure.

5. **Obligations, dates and deadlines** (1.5 pages)
   - Turning text into a tracked list with owner and due date; renewal and notice windows.
   - Human confirmation before reminders are relied upon.

6. **Risk flags** (1.5 pages)
   - Comparing clauses against a playbook your lawyers write; what a flag means and does not mean.
   - Why the playbook is the legal team's, not the vendor's.

7. **Question answering over a contract collection** (1.5 pages)
   - Retrieval with citations; example questions; what happens when the answer spans several contracts.
   - Access control: matter-level and client-level confidentiality.

8. **Confidentiality, privilege and deployment** (1.5 pages)
   - Where documents are processed; on-premise and private options; retention and deletion.
   - Consent and professional-conduct considerations for the reader to check with their regulator.

9. **Evaluation worksheet** (1.5 pages)
   - Build a test set of your contracts; define fields and clauses; measure per field; set review thresholds.

10. **Rolling it out** (1 page)
    - Start with one contract type; train reviewers; feedback loop.

11. **Questions to ask any vendor** (half page)

12. **About Quantum Atlas** and a link to book a discovery call at https://quantumatlas.dev/contact.
